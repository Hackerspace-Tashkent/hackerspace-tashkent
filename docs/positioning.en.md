# Who we are, what we have, and what we do not

> Written 30 September 2026. This is an honest description, not advertising.

## Starting with the uncomfortable part

**We have only just started. Almost nothing works yet.**

We have no members, no meetings that have happened, no completed hardware
project. Not one person has worked through our materials from beginning to end.
The progress board is empty — zero entries.

Below is an exact list of what exists, what has been verified, and what is only
text. Nothing is embellished.

If you join now, you are not joining a working community. You are joining a group
that is starting. The difference is that this group has some groundwork, even if
small, and that you can join it by having an effect rather than by watching.

## What already works and has been verified

These are not promises, they are counted numbers.

| What | How many | How it was verified |
|---|---|---|
| Labs with automatic checking | 8 | each run in two scenarios: "empty" and "done" |
| Automatic checks inside them | 67 | they really run, rather than "should run" |
| Lessons | 8 | across two levels: from zero, and confident user |
| Language versions | 76 | Russian, English, Uzbek in Latin, Uzbek in Cyrillic |
| Documents in `docs/` | 16 | including the safety guide and community standards |
| Result parsing for the board | 10 tests | forged and corrupted data is rejected |

What this means in practice: **the teaching material is built and works for a
developer.** All eight labs run, show exactly what did not work, and exit with
code 0 when everything is done. This has been verified many times.

The website, the material layout, and the consistency across four languages all
work too.

## What does not exist at all

Here is where the honest part starts.

- **Not a single member other than us.** None.
- **No meeting has ever happened.** The schedule "Saturday, 11:00" is a plan, not
  an event that took place. It has never been tested in practice.
- **Not a single hardware project.** The projects repository is empty apart from
  a licence and a readme.
- **Not a single workshop by someone who moved here.** Zero. The channel for
  them exists, and it is empty.
- **Not a single environmental project.** Zero, not "in progress".
- **No second level.** Intended, not written.
- **No board.** The code exists, the automation is blocked: our token has no
  permission for workflow files. Sending a result to the board does not work at
  the moment.
- **Not a single measurement of results.** How many people finished a level, how
  many found work afterwards — nobody knows, including us.

## What we do not know

This matters more than the list of absences. There is one main unknown:

> **Not one living person has been through these materials.**

We ran the checks ourselves. That proves the scripts work. It does **not** prove
that the material is understandable to someone who has never opened a terminal.

We do not know:

- whether a lesson is clear to someone with no technical background;
- whether it is boring to read;
- how long a lesson really takes;
- whether the explanations are enough or people get stuck;
- whether we picked the right order of topics.

This is not a flaw that can be fixed in advance. It is the only way to find out —
**give it to someone and watch where they stop.**

## What we do not promise

- **We do not promise it will all work.** Some of what is planned will not
  work out. That is normal and we will say so openly.
- **We do not promise jobs or certificates.** We have no such authority, and
  promising it would be a lie.
- **We do not promise every project will succeed.** Sometimes it does not.
- **We do not promise a closed club.** Interest is the entry requirement, not a
  record of service.

## What we offer instead of promises

We say plainly: **the teaching material is written, but has not been tested on a
person.** So we are not inviting students, we are inviting co-authors.

To someone who has never opened a terminal:

> **Do one lesson and tell us exactly where you got stuck.**

This is not a figure of speech and not a "please leave a review" request. It is
the most useful feedback possible, and we have nobody who can give it instead of
you.

To someone who can do something:

> **Run one lesson.**

Do not commit to writing the programme — teach one session. One. If you do not
want to continue, nothing bad happens. If you do, there will be something to
build on.

## Why we invite anyone when nothing is ready

Because members do not appear out of a finished product. They appear when they
can see the product being built with them.

Right now you can get what you cannot buy:

- **influence.** The material is 76 files — small, and every one is visible.
  Changing a lesson is a single PR.
- **real responsibility.** Not a role in an org chart, but work you do and are
  then answerable for.
- **a verifiable result.** You can write a lesson, a workshop, or a project that
  people will use.
- **credit.** Work done at the start keeps your name on it forever, in the git
  history.

## Who we especially need

**People living in Tashkent.** The material is free, works in a browser, needs
no hardware purchase, and exists in four languages including Uzbek in Cyrillic.
This is not available on the market here.

**People who have moved to Tashkent.** And here an important clarification:
by "relokants" we mean first and foremost **Uzbeks who came back from abroad** —
from Russia, Kazakhstan, Korea, Türkiye. They have experience and often money,
but no local network: you do not know who to go to or how anything works here.

That is exactly why there are four languages and not two: a person who thinks in
Uzbek reads in Russian. Two languages would have pushed them out of their own
environment.

> Someone who moved here arrives with skills but no local network. A local has a
> network but not always the skills. **Nobody is handing anything out.**

## What we are concretely asking for

| From whom | What |
|---|---|
| locals | two hours a week, finish one level, say where you got stuck |
| those who came back | one workshop, or help at a meeting |
| foreigners | the same: a workshop, help, or a note on how something here does not work |
| everyone | one meeting where we work out what to do next |

## Where we are going

The order we consider correct. Not promises about dates — order.

1. **A stranger does one lesson.** Until that happens, everything written is
   still a hypothesis.
2. **The first workshop by someone who moved here.** It will also be the second
   proof that the format works.
3. **The board starts filling.** It needs one permission in the token.
4. **The first hardware project.** An air quality sensor, if we decide to.
5. **The second level.** Written as a result, not in advance.

## If it does not work out

The material stays in an open repository under the MIT licence. It can be used,
translated, extended or thrown away. Even if the community does not form, a
Linux handbook in four languages that opens in a browser with nothing to install
is useful on its own.
